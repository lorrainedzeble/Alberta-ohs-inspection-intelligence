### 2026-07-10: same episode, fourth real fix, the back test gate itself had a gap

Before moving to Step 4, the analyst asked a direct question about the back test's own design: does `beats_random_sample_of_same_size` include any significance threshold, or is it comparing two raw rates directly? Checked the actual code (`inspection-target-ranking/script.py` line 157 at the time): `beats_random = top_rate > pop_rate`, a strict inequality on two observed rates, nothing else. No p-value, no confidence interval, no accounting for sampling variance at `top_n = 10`.

Computed the real exposure directly: the binomial chance probability of seeing at least as many top-10 hits by a purely random draw at the population's own rate, for the three weak-lift cases already flagged in the prior entry. Industrial/Commercial Construction: 18.4% chance at random. General Automotive Repairs/Auto Wreckers: 26.7%. Construction Framing Contractor: 17.8%. All three would have passed the old gate as "beats random sampling," language that implies more than a coin-flip-odds lucky draw.

**Fix applied:** the back test now requires both conditions before validating a ranking: `top_rate > pop_rate` (unchanged) AND `chance_probability_of_this_hit_rate_at_random < 0.05` (`BACKTEST_SIGNIFICANCE_THRESHOLD` in `inspection-target-ranking/script.py`, a disclosed, conventional threshold, not derived from this dataset). Documented in `knowledge/data-cautions.md`, `CLAUDE.md`'s gated exception and Success criteria, and `inspection-target-ranking/SKILL.md`.

### 2026-07-10: same episode, Step 4 (brief), fifth real fix

Ran `employer-briefing` on the top 3 ranked employers for each of the 10 validated industries, 30 briefings. Rather than accept the output, the analyst spot checked it and found two things directly in the raw JSON, neither hypothetical.

**Bug 1: a NaN lost-time-claim rate silently marked "stable."** Tidewater Midstream's 2024 entry showed `lost_time_claim_count: 0` but `lost_time_claim_rate: NaN` alongside `rate_stable: true`, a nonsensical combination, a rate that does not exist reported as a stable fact. Traced to `employer-briefing/script.py` line 70: `ohs.rate_from_counts(row["LOST_TIME_CLAIM_COUNT"] or 0, person_years)`. The `or 0` idiom is meant to substitute 0 for a missing (`NaN`) count, but `NaN` is truthy in Python, so `NaN or 0` evaluates to `NaN`, not `0`, and the substitution silently fails. `rate_from_counts` then computes `NaN / person_years`, which is `NaN`, while its stability check (`person_years >= 40`) is independent of the count and returns `True` regardless. A wider scan of all 30 briefs' full 5 year trends (not just the latest year) found this same pattern in 16 employer-year rows across 8 employers spanning 5 of the 10 industries: Boulder Metal Industries, Headwater Equipment Sales, Hahn Welding & Oilfield Services, Tidewater Midstream, Northwest Spring & Machine, Mr. Mike's Plumbing, and Petrin Mechanical.

**Fix applied:** replaced the `or 0` idiom with an explicit `pd_notna` check, the same pattern already used two lines below for the displayed count field, ensuring the same NaN never reaches the rate calculation as a truthy non-zero value. Reran all 30 briefs: zero NaN-rate rows remain. Every previously affected employer-year now shows a real rate, `0.0` for the genuine zero-claim years (for example Boulder Metal Industries 2020 to 2022, Tidewater Midstream 2024), and the pre-existing nonzero years are unchanged (Boulder Metal Industries 2023 still 16.077, 2024 still 38.85, both correctly still marked unstable given single-digit person years).

**Bug 2 (found while confirming Bug 1's fix): duplicate raw rows for one employer.** Northwest Spring & Machine's briefing showed every year's trend entry twice with identical values. Checked directly against the raw Injury sheet rather than assuming a script defect: confirmed real. `EMP_KEY 80ADDCBE-6258-F719-CC4E-81F42334B96D` has two rows per year, one labeled "NORTHWEST SPRING & MACHINE INC.," the other "TRUCK ZONE INC.," with identical `PERSON_YEARS_COUNT` and `LOST_TIME_CLAIM_COUNT` in every year. This is not an isolated case: 342 of 236,365 EMP_KEYs (0.145%) across the whole Injury sheet have more than one distinct `EMPLOYER_NAME`, and in every single one of the 342 (not a handful, all of them) the conflicting names appear within the *same year*, not as a rename over time. The pattern is consistent, a numbered corporation paired with an operating name (for example `01436142-BFD0-F247-77F5-269A483F8E51`: "2290682 ALBERTA LTD." / "OG DRYWALL LTD."), almost certainly a legal-name/trade-name pair sharing one WCB account, since `TRADE_NAME` itself is empty in this file.

This is materially worse than a display quirk: any code summing raw Injury rows without deduplicating by `EMP_KEY`+`YEAR_NO` double-counts these 342 employers, including `industry-trend-briefing`'s industry-level sums and `inspection-target-ranking`'s `groupby("EMP_KEY").agg(sum)` feature aggregation, both used throughout Steps 1 to 3 already run this session. It was invisible to the pinned Roofing gold example's own check, since the independent `pd.read_excel` recomputation summed the same undeduplicated rows the same way. Separately, `employer-briefing` and `inspection-target-ranking` each silently picked one name per `EMP_KEY` with no flag, despite CLAUDE.md's Governance section already stating "Escalate to the analyst on: EMP_KEY/name mismatches" as a rule, a real gap between a documented rule and the code that implements it.

Given the scope (touches the shared data loader every skill depends on), the analyst was asked directly how to handle it before proceeding rather than unilaterally reworking the pipeline: fix it properly now (re-verify the gold example, full scan, and all 15 backtests), fix narrowly (just the two skills already in play), or document only. **The analyst chose to fix it properly.**

**Fix applied:** `ohs_data.py`'s `load_injury()` now deduplicates on `(EMP_KEY, YEAR_NO)`, keeping one row; checked first that every numeric column is identical across the duplicate rows for all 1,288 same-EMP_KEY-same-year groups found (13 of these 1,288 groups additionally carry two different `CITY_NAME` values, a narrower, separately documented sub-caveat, not separately flagged in code given its scale of 13 employers). A new `ambiguous_employer_names()` function returns every name on file for the 342 affected EMP_KEYs. `employer-briefing` now surfaces `employer_name_ambiguous` and `employer_name_all_known_names`; `inspection-target-ranking` surfaces `employer_name_ambiguous` per shortlisted employer in both `rank()` and `backtest()`'s features. Full detail in `knowledge/data-cautions.md`.

**Verification, in order:**
1. Confirmed the dedup is safe: 100% of the 1,288 duplicate groups have identical injury/enforcement numbers across the name-variant rows (only `CITY_NAME` differs, and only for 13 of them).
2. Re-ran the pinned Roofing gold example: `person_years_sum` changed from 4921.3 to 4907.7, `ltc_rate_per_100_person_years` from 2.703 to 2.71, `di_rate_per_100_person_years` from 4.795 to 4.809. `employer_count` (1137), `ltc_count` (133), `di_count` (236), `fatality_count` (1), and `enforcement_action_count` (802) are unchanged, since the 4 duplicated EMP_KEYs within ROOFING/2020 (Perfect Slope Roofing/Construction, Rockyview Group/Roofing, Horizon Roofing/R. Ripley & Son Sheet Metal, Sean Britton/Sean R. Britton) all had zero/NaN claim and disabling-injury counts, only inflated person-years. Independently re-derived with a fresh, separately-written `pd.read_excel` script (1141 raw rows, 1137 after dedup): matched exactly. The pinned gold example in CLAUDE.md was updated to the corrected values with a note explaining the change.
3. Re-ran the full 302-industry scan: 302 scanned, 114 flagged, unchanged from before this fix, and the same 15 industries remain the top-15 by size in the same order. Individual rates shifted marginally across the dataset but no industry crossed a flag threshold as a result.
4. Re-ran all 15 previously-decided backtests: all 15 gate verdicts unchanged (10 validated, 5 rejected), same population sizes, same chance probabilities. The 342 affected employers evidently did not concentrate in these particular 15 industries' top-N or population sets enough to move any verdict.

This is the sixth real, independently-verified fix from this use episode: a bug that was small in scale (0.145% of employers) but structurally real, present in the shared data layer, invisible to the existing gold-example check precisely because both sides of that check shared the same blind spot, and only surfaced by an analyst spot-checking one specific employer's briefing rather than accepting the output.

### 2026-07-11: an adversarial critic actually operated the workspace, not just read its code

Everything above verified *numbers*. Nothing yet had tested whether the workspace, actually used live by someone role-playing Dana with a real question, gets her a usable answer. A critic was briefed on exactly that question and told to try to break the workspace by using it, not by reading the scripts.

**Method:** fresh context, no access to the reasoning or fixes above, no access to this file or the memo. Read only `CLAUDE.md` and what it points to, then actually ran the tools: a real `--all` scan, real `rank`/`backtest` calls on real flagged industries (General Trucking, Roofing), a real `enforcement-gap-watchlist` run, a real `employer-briefing` dossier. Told specifically to try to get the workspace to refuse something it claims to refuse, to try to skip the Step 2 confirmation pause, to check for governed-language and citation violations, and to report anywhere it got confused as a stranger would.

**Five real problems found, none hypothetical, all confirmed by direct reproduction:**
1. **`CITY_NAME` is a mailing address, not a confirmed Alberta worksite.** A live ranking of General Trucking (a real, large, fatality-flagged industry) grouped its top-10 shortlist under Mississauga, Richmond, Lachine, Winnipeg, and Brampton, one Alberta city among them. The file has no province or worksite field; checked directly, 2,516 distinct `CITY_NAME` values exist, and a robust auto-classifier against real Alberta municipalities isn't reliably buildable from this alone. **Fix:** an explicit, always-shown caveat in `inspection-target-ranking/SKILL.md` and CLAUDE.md, not a silent (and unreliable) auto-classification.
2. **Raw Excel date serials reached the analyst.** `employer-briefing`'s enforcement history showed `"ISSUE_DATE": 43902.5715625` for a real flagged employer (Flynn Canada Ltd.) instead of a date, because `_sheet_history()` read the raw column instead of the already-computed `ACTION_DATE` field. Independently converted (`2020-03-12`) to confirm it was a display bug, not a wrong date. **Fixed.**
3. **`enforcement-gap-watchlist` dropped the source sheet before it reached the analyst**, a direct citation-convention violation (`most_recent_enforcement_action_by_employer()` returns a source sheet; `watchlist()` discarded it before use). **Fixed:** `most_recent_enforcement_action_source_sheet` now included per flagged employer.
4. **Look-ahead leakage inside the ranking's own validation gate.** `unresolved_order_ever` and `acceptance_approval_concern`, two of five score components, ignored `as_of_year` entirely, always reading the full 2020-2024 Order/Acceptance/Approval sheets regardless of the scoring cutoff.
