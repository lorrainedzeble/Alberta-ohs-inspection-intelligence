# CLAUDE.md: Alberta OHS Inspection Prioritization Workspace

## User story brief
1. As Dana, I want to see which industries are trending the wrong way each quarter without first having to already know which ones are, so that I don't have to manually cross-reference 302 industries' injury and enforcement data by hand before I can even start deciding where to focus.
2. As Dana, I want a back-tested, ranked shortlist of specific employers within a confirmed industry, so that I can allocate this quarter's limited inspection capacity to the employers actually most likely to justify a visit, not just the largest or most familiar names.
3. As Dana, I want a one-page dossier on a specific employer before I visit them, so that I arrive already knowing their injury trend, enforcement history, and any Acceptance/Approval instruments on file, rather than discovering it during the visit.

**Story #1, interrogated:** is this a real want, or one invented to justify a tool? Alberta OHS's own published selection criteria (cited below) describe sector-level selection logic, but nothing in the public record hands Dana a pre-computed, employer-level or even industry-level ranking she can act on directly; the underlying raw material is a 302-industry, 841,761-row, 5-year workbook with four differently-formatted date fields across the enforcement sheets. Recomputing Step 1 by hand would mean pivoting five years of injury rates industry by industry, checking each against 2023-2024 fatality counts, and reconciling four separately-formatted enforcement sheets, before ranking a single employer. That manual cost is why this is the first decision this workspace automates, not a convenience feature bolted onto an invented narrative after the fact. The one honest caveat this story can't resolve: it assumes Dana doesn't already carry an informal mental model of "which industries are usually bad" from experience, and how much that prior knowledge already substitutes for Step 1's scan in practice was never actually tested with a real user, a limitation that should be tested with a real OHS planning analyst (see `knowledge/opportunity-framing.md`).

## Scope
In scope: the five decisions named below, for Dana specifically, using only the data in this one workbook. Out of scope, stated explicitly (separate from the ethical/data-quality refusals in the next section): any decision this workspace hasn't actually been asked to make, for example a flat cross-industry employer ranking (considered and rejected, see `knowledge/dashboard-mockup-framing.md`'s shape 3 rationale); any user other than Dana, this is not a public tool, a multi-analyst tool, or built for a different OHS branch; and any data source beyond this one workbook, no external live connector exists or is planned (see `knowledge/capability-map.md`'s connector row, left empty on purpose).

## Purpose
**Objective, stated without naming a single column (the no-dataset sentence):** decide which industries deserve this quarter's proactive inspection attention, and which specific employers within them justify an actual visit. This sentence would still be true if the underlying workbook were replaced entirely; everything below it is how *this* dataset happens to answer it.

This workspace exists for one user: **Dana**, a compliance planning analyst in Alberta OHS's Prevention Services branch. Every Monday morning, before that week's inspection assignments go out, Dana opens this workspace to make that decision. This isn't an invented job. It operationalizes Alberta OHS's own published selection logic:

> "Each year OHS chooses particular industrial sectors to proactively inspect, selecting sectors that have relatively high injury and illness rates, high frequency of incidents or complaints, persistently low rates of compliance, or are emerging trends." OHS officers focus on worksites "identified by OHS as chronically noncompliant."
> Source: [alberta.ca/ohs-proactive-inspection-program](https://www.alberta.ca/ohs-proactive-inspection-program)

This lets Dana decide, in an afternoon instead of days of manual cross-referencing, which employers among roughly 236,000 WCB-registered firms actually deserve this quarter's inspection attention, a decision she has no reliable way to make at that scale without it. Those four public criteria (injury rate, incident/complaint frequency, compliance history, emerging trend) are exactly what the five skills below compute, just at the employer level instead of the sector level, using the actual WCB injury data plus OHS Act Part 7 enforcement records (orders, penalties, tickets, investigations, convictions) this dataset contains. Each skill answers Dana's question at a different point in the quarter:

- "Where should we be looking?" leads to industry trend briefing
- "Given that focus, who specifically?" leads to inspection target ranking
- "I'm about to visit this employer, brief me." leads to employer briefing
- "Did our last enforcement action actually work?" leads to enforcement effectiveness
- "Who's climbing in risk but never shown up on our enforcement radar?" leads to enforcement gap watchlist

Not a general purpose OHS data explorer. If a request doesn't map to one of these five decisions, it's out of scope. Say so rather than answering from raw data ad hoc.

Six other framings were considered and lost to this one on locked criteria, written down in full in `knowledge/opportunity-framing.md`, including two (COR effectiveness, a public leaderboard) that were rejected for reasons that later hardened into refusals below.

## What this workspace refuses to do (dated 2026.07.09)
1. Does not make any COR claim at all, not even "held in year X." `COR_YEAR` is not just dateless, it is completely empty in this file (0 of 841,761 Injury rows populated, checked directly with pandas on 2026.07.09). There is no COR data to work with in this extract. Superseded entirely by enforcement gap watchlist, which uses data that exists.
2. Does not produce a public "worst offenders" leaderboard. This is internal capacity allocation support for Dana specifically, not a public accountability tool. Naming employers for public consumption is a different problem with different ethical stakes and is out of scope.
3. Does not forecast whether or when a specific employer will have a future fatality or injury. Outputs are trend and history based risk signals, never a predictive probability claim.
4. Does not treat Acceptance/Approval expiry dates as a live status signal. Their `DESCRIPTION` status (Issued, Expired, Revoked, Denied, Suspended) is not reliably synced to `EXPIRY_DATE` (see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`), so no skill infers current validity or an "expiring soon" state from these dates, the same reasoning that killed the original COR expiry idea. They are used narrowly instead: as historical context in employer briefing, and as one supplementary signal in inspection target ranking, never as a primary driver and never date derived.
5. Does not surface free text narrative fields (Investigation description beyond its flag use, Penalty nature of violation prose, Conviction description/contravention prose). These exist in the data but the analyst's five decisions do not need them; showing them would be exposing a field because it exists, not because the decision needs it.

## Data (Knowledge)
Source: `2024_ohs-employer-record-open-data.xlsx` (Government of Alberta open data, employer level OHS/WCB records, 2020 to 2024), connected live from `data/` (a copy of the source file lives there). Skills query the source file directly. Nothing gets duplicated into narrative docs. Knowledge folder layout:
- `knowledge/data-dictionary.md`: field names and types, built from the live sheet headers (the vendor docx is stale on key field names).
- `knowledge/data-cautions.md`: known traps (COR, Acceptance/Approval date sync, Investigation's missing date field, rate suppression, join key stability). Read before writing any skill logic.
- `knowledge/data-profile.md`: real coverage numbers (row counts, distributions, overlap between sheets).
- `knowledge/domain-notes.md`: OHS/WCB vocabulary, formulas, and the citation grounding this workspace's persona.

Eight sheets, all joinable on `EMP_KEY`:
- Injury (841,761 rows): one row per employer per year. Person years, lost time claim count/rate, disabling injury count/rate, fatality counts, industry, city. Also has a `COR_YEAR` column and a `TRADE_NAME` column, both completely empty in this file; not used anywhere.
- Order (71,755 rows): Stop Work Orders. Issue date, legislation code, contravention, order type, status.
- Penalty, Ticket, Conviction: dated enforcement actions.
- Investigation (91 rows): description plus URL only, no date column. A binary "ever investigated" flag only, never date windowed logic.
- Acceptance (909 rows, 234 employers), Approval (253 rows, 83 employers): used narrowly. See refusal 4 above and `knowledge/data-cautions.md`.

Known data realities. Surface these, never work around them silently; full detail in `knowledge/data-cautions.md`:
- Rates are not meaningful under 40 person years (per the official dictionary). Flag, don't report as a stable fact.
- `EMP_KEY`, not employer name, is the join key. Flag (don't silently drop) any name that maps to multiple keys or vice versa.
- Acceptance/Approval status is not reliably synced to its own expiry date; never infer current validity from it.
- `COR_YEAR` and `TRADE_NAME` are completely empty in this file (0 of 841,761 rows populated). Not a formatting issue, there is no data present at all; no skill may reference either field.
- 342 of 236,365 `EMP_KEY` values (0.145%) are recorded under two different `EMPLOYER_NAME` values in the same year, a legal-name/trade-name pair sharing one WCB account. `load_injury()` deduplicates by `(EMP_KEY, YEAR_NO)` to prevent double-counting; `employer-briefing` and `inspection-target-ranking` both surface an `employer_name_ambiguous` flag rather than silently picking one name, per the EMP_KEY/name-mismatch escalation rule below. See `knowledge/data-cautions.md`.

## Skills
Self contained, repeatable. Same inputs produce the same output shape every time. All five read the live source and the dictionary; none hardcode numbers. The verb-by-verb reasoning behind each form choice below (skill vs. subagent vs. workflow vs. connector left empty) is written out in full in `knowledge/capability-map.md`.

1. industry trend briefing: industry in, 5 year LTC/DI rate, fatality, and enforcement volume trend out, flagged if moving the wrong way. Rates are computed from summed raw counts across all employers in the industry year, never averaged from individual employer rates, since 93% of individual employer year rows fall under the 40 person year stability threshold (see `knowledge/data-profile.md`).
2. inspection target ranking: industry/region focus in, risk ranked shortlist out, grouped by the employer's registered mailing city (not a confirmed Alberta worksite, see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`), score components always shown (including, when present, a revoked/denied/suspended Acceptance or Approval as a supplementary flag), never just rank. Weights, disclosed here and never hidden a layer down: recent LTC count ×1.0, recent DI count ×1.0, fatality-ever ×5.0, unresolved-order-ever ×3.0, Acceptance/Approval concern ×1.0 (`inspection-target-ranking/script.py`'s `WEIGHTS` dict is the single source of truth; change them there, not in the analyst's head). An unresolved Order is defined explicitly as `STATUS in ("Non-Compliance", "Open")`, never a vague "not yet complied with," since 98.5% of all Order rows are STATUS "Compliance" and would otherwise be miscounted as unresolved; see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`. Industry is attributed via the EMP_KEY join back to Injury's WCB_INDUSTRY_NAME as the primary source; Penalty's own INDUSTRY_SECTOR is used only as a fallback for the 15% of Penalty employers with no Injury row, since it has been verified consistent wherever both exist (see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`). A shortlist can carry its own boundary tie (multiple employers sharing the exact cutoff score) independently of whether the industry's back test overall validated, rejected, or deferred; several of the ten validated industries in the corrected 2026-07-13 cycle still have a tied slot below their confirmed top employers. Any presentation layer (a dashboard, a future UI) must show the unambiguous and tied employers separately, per `at_top_n_boundary_tie` and `knowledge/data-cautions.md`, not silently pick one.
3. employer briefing: EMP_KEY or name in, one page dossier out: trend vs. benchmark, full enforcement history, legislation checklist, and any Acceptance/Approval instruments on record shown as historical status, not live validity.
4. enforcement effectiveness: EMP_KEY or industry in, injury/DI rate before vs. after first enforcement action out, actual numbers shown, not just a verdict. First enforcement action is the minimum of Order.ISSUE_DATE, Ticket.OFFENCE_DATE, Penalty.SERVED_DATE, and Conviction.DATE_OF_CONVICTION per EMP_KEY, normalized to real dates, never Conviction.INCIDENT_DATE. Compares up to 2 years before and up to 2 years after the action year, using whatever years exist in 2020 to 2024; if either side has zero available years for a given employer, the output says "insufficient data, before/after not computable" for that employer rather than showing a number. Full detail in `knowledge/data-cautions.md`.
5. enforcement gap watchlist: rising injury trend (2+ years up) and no Order/Penalty/Ticket/Conviction in 3+ years, defined as the most recent action date (the maximum of the same unified date table enforcement effectiveness uses, see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`) being more than 3 years old, or absent entirely; "ever investigated" tracked as a separate flag.

Cross cutting: `validate-metric` defines an independent-verification role (`.claude/agents/validate-metric.md`). Because this runtime does not support it as a registered, automatically-dispatched subagent type (confirmed directly on 2026.07.14: invoking it by name is rejected outright, see `evaluation/deployment-assessment.md`), it is invoked manually: its role instructions are handed to a general-purpose agent alongside a skill's draft output, with no access to the producing skill's reasoning, and it recomputes every number independently from the raw sheets, stamping each PASS/FAIL. A FAIL blocks that output and escalates to the analyst; the verdict, PASS or FAIL, and what was corrected if any, is logged in `evaluation/use-log.md` the same day, a required step, not an optional afterthought (see Governance). This is a deliberate operating procedure that must be run explicitly on every skill invocation, not a built-in automatic gate, the same honest distinction drawn for `/quarterly-cycle`'s Step 2 pause below.

Workflow: `/quarterly-cycle` (`.claude/commands/quarterly-cycle.md`) strings skills 1 through 3 together into one consolidated memo instead of four separate outputs. It scans every industry in one pass (industry-trend-briefing's `--all` mode, one workbook load, originally verified at about 143 seconds for all 302 industries, not 302 relaunches; this figure could not be reproduced on 2026-07-14, see `GETTING_STARTED.md`'s reconciled benchmark table, which measured roughly 8-9 minutes for the same `--all` scan and flags the discrepancy rather than silently resolving it), presents the largest flagged industries to the analyst, and stops. Only after the analyst confirms or adjusts that list does it proceed to rank and brief the confirmed set, running `validate metric` on every number before assembling the memo. This keeps industry trend briefing's own purpose intact (the analyst should not need to already know which industries are trending badly before running the tool that tells them that) without ever letting the workflow pick industries and run straight through with no human touchpoint. Logs each run to `evaluation/use-log.md`.

## Conventions
- Recompute rates/trends from raw counts every time. Never estimate from memory.
- Every output that names an employer cites `EMP_KEY`, year(s), and source sheet(s).
- Every ranked/flagged list shows why (components/thresholds), not just the result.
- Missing field or date: say so explicitly, never approximate.
- Full automatic fail list and governed language rules live in `governance/governance-rules.md`.

## Governance
- The analyst makes the final call, not the workspace. No skill's output is a decision. Every ranking, briefing, or watchlist entry is a signal for the analyst to weigh, and requires the analyst's sign off before it results in an inspection, audit, or any action toward a named employer. This workspace supports the decision; it does not make it.
- Agent computes/ranks/drafts autonomously; `validate-metric`'s role gates every output, but only when the operating agent explicitly runs that manual step (see Skills section above) — this runtime does not enforce it automatically.
- Agent never states an enforcement recommendation as fact. Signals for the analyst to weigh, not conclusions (full automatic fails list in `governance/governance-rules.md`).
- Gated exceptions, written in advance:
  - If enforcement gap watchlist flags more than 15% of employers in a scope, that's a threshold calibration problem, not a finding. Say so, don't return an oversized list as if it were meaningful.
  - If inspection target ranking's back test (see Success criteria) doesn't beat random sampling, or the gap is not statistically significant at the 0.05 level (see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`), the ranking formula is rejected and must be revised, or shipped only as "sorted by raw indicators," never as a risk score.
  - If the back test's top-N cutoff score is tied across more than one employer (`top_n_boundary_tied: true`), the verdict is **not** set from the tie flag alone. Both ends of the range are computed: the worst-case tie-break (tied slots filled with as few 2023-2024 enforcement hits as possible) and the best-case tie-break (filled with as many hits as possible). The industry is **validated** only if even the worst-case resolution still clears the significance bar, **rejected** only if even the best-case resolution still fails it, and **deferred** only if the verdict genuinely flips between those two ends, since only then does which employers land in the top N actually change the substantive conclusion rather than just the arithmetic. A tie at the boundary is common; a tie that changes the verdict is rare, and only the second kind should ever produce "deferred."
    - Found live (2026.07.12): 9 of 15 industries scanned in one real cycle had a boundary tie (`employers_tied_at_top_n_boundary > 1`). The first implementation deferred all 9 on the tie flag alone, a bug discovered and fixed on 2026.07.13: re-deriving the worst-case/best-case range for each showed only 1 of the 9 (Construction Framing Contractor, worst-case chance probability 45.3%, best-case 1.0%) is genuinely tie-sensitive; the other 8 have a real but immaterial tie and resolve cleanly regardless of which tie-break is used (4 validated: Welding, Upstream Oil/Gas, Mobile Equipment Dealers, Oilfield Downhole Services; 4 rejected: Electric Wiring, Industrial/Commercial Construction, Field Production Operators, General Automotive Repairs/Auto Wreckers; see `evaluation/use-log.md` for the full per-industry breakdown). Corrected cycle result: 10 validated, 4 rejected, 1 deferred, not the originally reported 6 validated, 9 deferred, 0 rejected.
- Escalate to the analyst on: EMP_KEY/name mismatches, schema drift from what's documented here, requests outside the five skills, any `validate metric` FAIL.
- Every override is logged, not silently applied. If Dana rejects or overrides a flagged finding, a ranking, or a watchlist entry, that override and her stated reason get written into `evaluation/use-log.md` the same day, the same way a critic's finding gets a written verdict. A decision made against the workspace's output is exactly as much a record as one made with it.
- Every `validate-metric` verdict is logged, not just acted on. PASS or FAIL, and if FAIL, what was found and what was corrected, gets written into `evaluation/use-log.md` the same day that check is run. This is a required step of the manual validate-metric procedure (see Skills section), not an optional afterthought; a FAIL that gets fixed but never logged is exactly the "fix-committed-but-not-recorded" gap this workspace's own evaluation is supposed to catch.
- **Known limitation, narrowed on 2026-07-14 but not eliminated:** `/quarterly-cycle`'s Step 2 confirmation pause was, until 2026-07-14, an instruction to the operating agent only, not a code-level lock; a live adversarial test on 2026-07-11 confirmed it could be walked through with zero human input and still produce a fully "validated" memo. Step 3 now runs through `.claude/skills/_lib/run_quarterly_steps.py`, which requires a non-empty `--confirmation` value and refuses to run at all (exit code 1, no workbook access) if it is empty or omitted; whatever the analyst actually says is logged to `evaluation/use-log.md` automatically, before any ranking work happens. This closes the "walked through with literally nothing" version of the gap, but it cannot verify that the confirmation text represents a genuine, considered analyst decision rather than a meaningless placeholder typed only to pass the check. Anyone evaluating this workspace's governance should test this seam directly, not take this prose on faith either way.
- **Same class of limitation, confirmed 2026-07-14:** `validate-metric` is likewise an instruction, not a code-level lock — this runtime rejects it outright as a registered subagent type, confirmed by direct attempted invocation, not assumed. Every claim in this file that it "runs on," "gates," or "checks" an output means the operating agent must explicitly perform that manual step (hand its role instructions to a general-purpose agent), not that the workspace enforces it automatically. Tested directly on 2026-07-14: a deliberately corrupted number was correctly caught and FAILed when the manual step was actually run, confirming the procedure works when followed, not that it happens on its own.

## What good looks like
- Dana runs `/quarterly-cycle` Monday morning and, by Step 2's confirmation checkpoint, already knows which industries deserve attention this quarter without having pre-decided the answer herself.
- Every number she sees traces to a source sheet and a year; every ranked list shows her why an employer is on it, not just that it is.
- When the data can't support a clean answer (a boundary tie, an insufficient-data case, an industry with almost no enforcement activity), the workspace says so instead of forcing a false-precision answer.
- She never has to trust a claim she can't check herself; the pinned gold example and `validate-metric`'s independent recomputation mean any number here is falsifiable, not just asserted.
- What good does **not** look like: a longer report, a fancier dashboard, or a more comprehensive data explorer. If a change doesn't move Dana closer to one of her three stories above, it's out of scope regardless of how easy it would be to add.

### Output acceptance checklist
- [ ] It answers one of the three named decisions: where to look, who warrants review, or what to know before a visit.
- [ ] Every named employer includes `EMP_KEY`, year(s), and source sheet(s).
- [ ] Every composite score exposes its components and the weights used.
- [ ] Any rate based on fewer than 40 person-years is marked unstable.
- [ ] Missing, stale, or ambiguous data is surfaced rather than guessed or silently dropped.
- [ ] Ranking language matches the back-test verdict: validated, rejected, or deferred.
- [ ] Independent verification status is explicit: draft, pending, passed, failed, or passed after correction.
- [ ] The output uses governed language and leaves the operational decision with the analyst.

## Success criteria (testable)
- industry trend briefing and employer briefing: figures reproduce exactly against independent manual recomputation.
- inspection target ranking: top N shortlist, back tested (ranked on 2020 to 2022 data, checked against 2023 to 2024 enforcement outcomes), beats a random sample of the same size on enforcement action rate, and that gap is statistically significant at the 0.05 level, not just numerically higher (see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`).
- enforcement effectiveness: correctly finds each employer's first enforcement action across all four dated enforcement sheets and computes before/after windows with no off by one errors; correctly reports "insufficient data" rather than a number whenever either side of the window has zero available years.
- enforcement gap watchlist: zero false positives on spot check against the Order/Penalty/Ticket/Conviction sheets directly.
- Evaluation is run before and after at least one real revision, both readings kept in the project record, every finding given a human verdict (accept, reject, or defer) with a reason. Logged in `evaluation/use-log.md`; the same history is also indexed as a scannable table in `evaluation/before-after-table.md`, including the claims that were checked and found false, not just the ones that turned out to be real bugs.
