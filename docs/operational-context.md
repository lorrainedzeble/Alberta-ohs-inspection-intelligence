# Operational context

The opportunity is not simply “analyze a large workbook.” It is to support scarce inspection capacity with consistent, reviewable triage.

## Published scale

The Government of Alberta reported the following OHS activity for 2023–24:

| Measure | 2022–23 | 2023–24 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inspections conducted | 9,571 | 9,801 |
| Re-inspections conducted | 4,146 | 5,975 |
| OHS orders written | 9,099 | 12,594 |
| Unique worksites visited | 7,092 | 7,548 |
| New businesses contacted | 12,342 | 14,603 |

Thirty-five percent of inspections were proactive and 65% reactive. Applying those published shares to 9,801 inspections gives an approximate planning context of 3,430 proactive inspections and 6,371 reactive inspections. These derived figures are directional because the annual report publishes rounded percentages.

The report explicitly connects the increase in inspection and re-inspection activity to an operational shift toward evidence-based, intelligence-led inspection planning.

## What the workspace changes

The workspace does **not** estimate staffing, officer hours, budget savings, or avoided injuries because the public sources reviewed do not provide a defensible conversion from one ranked employer to those outcomes.

It does make the proactive selection stage more reproducible:

- one scan across all industries;
- one explicit analyst confirmation seam;
- one statistically gated employer shortlist within each confirmed industry;
- one pre-visit briefing that keeps source citations and data cautions attached.

The leverage claim is therefore bounded: this system can reduce repeated manual cross-referencing and make triage auditable; it does not prove that its recommendations improve safety outcomes without a real operational pilot.
