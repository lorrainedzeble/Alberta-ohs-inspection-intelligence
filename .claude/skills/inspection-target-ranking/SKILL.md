name: inspection-target-ranking
description: Produces a risk ranked shortlist of specific employers within a chosen industry and/or city, grouped by city for routing, with every score component disclosed. ## When to use this
The analyst's "given that focus, who specifically" question, the second moment in CLAUDE.md's quarterly cycle. Comes after a focus has been chosen (an industry, a city, or both); this skill does not choose the focus itself.

## How to run it
Ranking: `python script.py rank --industry "INDUSTRY NAME" --city "CITY NAME" --top-n 20 --as-of-year 2024` from this folder. `--industry` and `--city` are each optional and independent (omit either to skip filtering on it); use named flags, not positional empty strings, PowerShell silently drops empty-string positional arguments and shifts every argument after it.

Back test: `python script.py backtest --industry "INDUSTRY NAME" --city "CITY NAME" --top-n 20`. Scores employers using only 2020 to 2022 data, then checks what fraction of the top N actually had an enforcement action in 2023 to 2024, against the same rate across the whole eligible population.

The back test requires two things to hold, not one, before a ranking is validated: the top-N rate must exceed the population rate (`beats_random_sample_of_same_size`), and that gap must be unlikely to occur by chance (`significant`, a binomial P(X>=observed hits) under the population's own rate, must fall below `significance_threshold`, 0.05). A raw rate comparison alone is not enough at small `top_n`, where a handful of hits can clear the population rate by chance; see `knowledge/data-cautions.md` for the concrete case that surfaced this (three industries whose top-10 rate technically beat the population rate but had an 18-27% chance of doing so at random).

**Before presenting any ranked output, run the back test for the same industry/city scope if it has not been run recently, and check `verdict`.** Three possible values, never collapsed into a single pass/fail: `"validated"` (present as a risk ranking; if `top_n_boundary_tied` is also true, the tie exists but even its worst-case resolution still clears the bar), `"rejected"` (present only as "sorted by raw indicators," cite `chance_probability_of_this_hit_rate_at_random`; if `top_n_boundary_tied` is also true, even the best-case resolution still fails), or `"deferred_boundary_tie"` (`tie_sensitive: true`; the verdict genuinely flips between the worst-case and best-case tie resolution, see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`). A tied boundary alone does not force a deferred verdict, only a tie that actually changes the conclusion does (checked directly, 2026.07.13: 9 of 15 industries in one real cycle had a boundary tie, but only 1 was genuinely tie-sensitive). A deferred result is not the same as rejected: say so explicitly, do not fold it into either the validated or rejected bucket, and do not present it as a clean answer either way until the tie is resolved by a methodology decision, not a coin flip.

## How to present it
- Always show the score components (`recent_ltc_count`, `recent_di_count`, `fatality_ever`, `unresolved_order_ever`, `acceptance_approval_concern`) alongside the total score for every employer shown, never a bare rank.
- State plainly that the score is built from raw counts, not rates, and therefore favors higher volume employers in absolute terms; this is a disclosed design choice (see `script.py`'s docstring and `knowledge/data-profile.md`), not a hidden bias to gloss over.
- Group the shortlist by city, as returned in `shortlist_by_city`, for routing. **`CITY_NAME` is the employer's registered WCB mailing address, not a confirmed Alberta worksite location** (there is no province or worksite field in this dataset, see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`). For large or multi-location employers this can be an out-of-province head office (a real example found in use: a national trucking company's shortlist entries grouped under Mississauga, Richmond, and Lachine, none in Alberta). State this caveat every time a shortlist is presented, and tell the analyst to confirm the employer's actual Alberta worksite location(s) before routing an inspection, this shortlist's city grouping does not do that confirmation for her.
- `unresolved_order_ever` means the employer has at least one Order with STATUS "Non-Compliance" or "Open," never the 98.5% majority "Compliance" status; see `knowledge/data-cautions.md` if asked why.
- Cite `EMP_KEY` for every employer named, per CLAUDE.md's Conventions.
- If `employer_name_ambiguous` is true for a shortlisted employer, say so and point to `employer-briefing` for the full list of names on file (342 of 236,365 employers are recorded under more than one name for the same `EMP_KEY`, see `knowledge/data-cautions.md`); never present `EMPLOYER_NAME` here as the only name that account operates under.
- Every employer on this list is a signal for the analyst to weigh, never a recommendation to inspect stated as fact, per CLAUDE.md's Governance.
- **Check `top_n_boundary_tied` and each employer's `at_top_n_boundary_tie`.** Present employers with `at_top_n_boundary_tie: false` as a normal ranked list, they are unambiguous, no one outside the shortlist shares their exact score. Present employers with `at_top_n_boundary_tie: true` as a separate, explicitly-labeled tied group ("N employers tied at score X, any could equally occupy this slot"), never folded into the ranked list as if their specific rank order or presence were meaningful; `employers_tied_at_top_n_boundary` states how many employers share that score. This matters most for a `deferred_boundary_tie` industry (see CLAUDE.md's Governance), where the tied group is the entire reason the industry's validation is deferred, but apply the same split even for a `validated` industry if its shortlist happens to carry a boundary tie too.

## Conventions this skill must follow
- Weights live in `script.py`'s `WEIGHTS` dict, not in this skill's prose. If the analyst wants different weights, change them there and note the change and the reason, don't improvise a different number in the response.
- Every output from this skill must be reviewed by `validate-metric` before being shown to the analyst as final.
